## Executive-skills track

The non-technical upgrade runs on a weekly practice loop, not on reading: every week produces one written brief and, every other week, one recorded talk, each scored against a fixed rubric so the 5/10 becomes measurable. Books supply the frameworks; the deliverables build the skill.

**The weekly loop (4 hrs):**

1. **Write** one page (Mon–Wed, 90 min): a brief on something real from the program or from work, in the format of the week. Rotate: decision memo, status update to a CIO, risk assessment, proposal, post-incident review.
2. **Speak** (every other week, 60 min): record a 5-minute talk on the same topic to a non-technical listener. Watch it back once. Score it.
3. **Score** (30 min): rubric below, 1–5 per line. Track in the program log. Target: average 4 by month 6.
4. **Deliver** (monthly): one live audience. Toastmasters, a team meeting at Compassus, a meetup, or a recorded post.

**Rubric (same for writing and speaking):**

| Criterion | 1 | 5 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answer first | The point arrives at the end, if at all | First sentence states the recommendation and the number |
| Audience fit | Written for another engineer | Written for the person who signs the check: cost, risk, time, options |
| Structure | Stream of consciousness | Situation → complication → question → answer; three supporting points, no more |
| Specificity | Adjectives ("significant", "robust") | Numbers, dates, names |
| Length | Everything you know | Only what the decision needs; one page or five minutes |
| Delivery (spoken) | Fillers, monotone, reading | Pauses, eye contact, one idea per breath |

**How C-level thinks (the lane most engineers skip):**

- Executives decide on three axes: money (cost, revenue, margin), risk (regulatory, security, reputational), and time (how long until it pays off or hurts). Every brief maps the technical thing onto those three.
- They want options with a recommendation, not a single answer and not a menu. Three options, one recommended, with what it costs to be wrong.
- They read the first paragraph and the last; they skim the middle for numbers.
- Resources: *Financial Intelligence* (Berman & Knight) for how they read numbers; *Smart Brevity and Minto's SCQA framework* for how they want to be told things; *The Trusted Advisor* (Maister, Green, Galford) for how to be the person they call, which is also the consulting playbook; *Measure What Matters* (Doerr) for how goals cascade down to you.

**Project planning and execution:** this program is the practice project. Run it with a written plan (this doc), a weekly review every Sunday, a monthly retrospective, a visible task list, and a changelog. Learn *Shape Up* (Basecamp, free online) for appetite-based scoping and the *PMI PMBOK* vocabulary only enough to speak it in interviews. The ability to say "I planned a 9-month program, hit the gates, and here is the retro" is itself an interview story.
